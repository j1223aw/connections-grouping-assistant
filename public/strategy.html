Why Puzzle Creators Try to Trick You (And How to Win)

The Connections puzzle is designed to be playful and a little sneaky. The creators love using common words that have two completely different meanings (like BAT for baseball or an animal with wings, or BARK for a dog or tree).

When you first look at the grid, your eye naturally spots words that seem related. The creators anticipate this! By placing five words that share a theme (such as BAT, AIRPLANE, BIRD, BUTTERFLY, and ANGEL all having wings), they create a 5-word trap. Guessing right away risks wasting one of your 4 allowed mistakes.

Our Golden Rule: Never submit a group the moment you see 4 matching words. Always look over all 16 tiles to see if there is a 5th word hiding. If there is, pause and look for another group first!

A Quick Guide to the 4 Difficulty Levels

Knowing what each color represents helps you understand how words connect and which words to save for harder groups:

🟨 LEVEL 1 • YELLOW

Words That Mean the Same Thing (Synonyms)

The most straightforward tier. All four words share clear dictionary definitions (like LEAP, BOUND, SPRING, and HOP). Yellow rarely relies on wordplay, but watch out: creators often place a 5th word that looks like it belongs here but is secretly needed for Purple.

🟩 LEVEL 2 • GREEN

Connected by Theme or Setting (Contextual Link)

Words linked by a shared theme or environment rather than direct definitions (like CAPTAIN, CREW, SKIPPER, and MATE on a ship). Think about where you would see these items together in real life.

🟦 LEVEL 3 • BLUE

Categories, Trivia & Words That Share a Word

Blue tests general knowledge, pop culture, or words that can all pair with another common word (like words ending in "BOARD": DASH, KEY, SURF, and SNOW).

🟪 LEVEL 4 • PURPLE

Letter & Spelling Tricks (Lateral Wordplay)

The trickiest level. Purple ignores dictionary definitions entirely! Instead, it plays with letters, sounds, or spelling (like words that sound like coins, words spelled backward, or words with five of the same vowel).

4 Simple Habits to Solve Any Board

  1. Read All 16 Words Out Loud First: Before clicking any tile, read through the whole board. Identify words that can mean more than one thing (like DATE as a fruit, calendar day, or romantic outing).
  2. Spot Words with Only One Obvious Meaning: Words with unique or unusual definitions help anchor the puzzle and narrow down your choices.
  3. Pause on 5-Word Groups: If you find 5 words matching one theme, don't guess yet. Look around the board to see which word is needed for another group.
  4. Lock In the Cleanest Group First: Solving one group right away leaves only 12 tiles, making the remaining puzzle much simpler to see!

33-Puzzle Data Study: What Actually Makes Purple Hard? (Oct–Nov 2026)

We analyzed 33 consecutive NYT Connections puzzles (#1220 through #1252, covering 528 total tiles from Oct 13 to Nov 14, 2026) to measure how often each type of Purple trap appears—and whether multi-word clues actually signal difficulty.

  • 51.5% Letter & Spelling Mechanics (17 of 33): Over half of all Purple groups rely on hidden words, letter swaps, palindromes, or vowel counts—such as BABY GEAR WITH FIRST LETTER CHANGED (BATTLE, MARRIAGE, MOTTLE, TRIB) and WORDS WITH FIVE OF ONE VOWEL (BEEKEEPER).
  • 21.2% Fill-in-the-Blank (7 of 33): Classic compound bridges like BRAIN ___ (FREEZE, TEASER, TRUST, WAVES) and FIRE___.
  • 18.2% Lateral Trivia & Double Meanings (6 of 33): Cultural associations that cross domain boundaries.
  • 9.1% Homophones & Sound-Alikes (3 of 33): Words linked purely by pronunciation, such as VEGETABLE HOMOPHONES (CARAT, CHARRED, KORN, LEAK).
  • Multi-Word Clues Are NOT a Purple Tell: Out of 78 multi-word or hyphenated tiles across 33 boards, distribution was nearly identical across every tier: 20 Yellow, 19 Green, 19 Blue, and 20 Purple.

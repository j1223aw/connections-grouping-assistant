How the 4 Colors Work: From Easiest to Trickiest

In the New York Times Connections puzzle, words aren't meant to be obscure or rare. You won't need a medical degree or a specialized dictionary. Instead, puzzle creators make the game fun and challenging by using common words that have more than one meaning (like BARK for a dog or a tree, or DATE for a fruit or calendar day).

As you move from Yellow to Purple, the connection shifts from straightforward dictionary definitions to broader themes, trivia, and clever wordplay:

🟨 YELLOW TIER • LEVEL 1 (EASIEST)

Words That Mean the Same Thing (Synonyms)

Yellow groups are the standard starting point. All four words share the same basic dictionary meaning—such as four words meaning fast (RUN, DASH, SPRINT, BOLT) or four pieces of furniture (CHAIR, SOFA, STOOL, BENCH).

Watch Out: Sometimes puzzle creators put five words that mean "fast," but one of them (like BOLT) is secretly needed for a trickier group ("Things with nuts and bolts"). Always double-check if there is a 5th word before you submit!

🟩 GREEN TIER • LEVEL 2 (MODERATE)

Connected by Theme or Subject (Contextual Link)

Green steps beyond dictionary synonyms. The words don't mean the same thing, but they naturally share a common setting, hobby, or subject area.

Examples: Tools in a woodworking shop (CHISEL, PLANE, ROUTER, VISE) or terms from high school English class (HYPERBOLE, OXYMORON, METAPHOR, SIMILE). Picture where you would encounter these words in real life.

🟦 BLUE TIER • LEVEL 3 (CHALLENGING)

Categories, General Knowledge & Shared Words (Trivia)

Blue tests fun general knowledge, pop culture, history, or words that can all pair with another common word.

Common Pattern: Words that can all come before or after another word (for example, words that pair with "JACK": APPLE, BLACK, CRACKER, LUMBER). When words seem unrelated, try pairing them with everyday words like "BOARD", "LIGHT", or "CARD".

🟪 PURPLE TIER • LEVEL 4 (TRICKIEST / WORDPLAY)

Letter & Spelling Tricks (Lateral Wordplay)

Purple is the most creative tier. It completely ignores what the words mean in the dictionary! Instead, it plays with how words are spelled, how they sound, or how they look:

  • Sounds Alike (Homophones): Words pronounced the same as other items (like CENT, SCENT, SENT).
  • Spelling Patterns: Words that all contain five of the same vowel (like today's ABRACADABRA with 5 A's and BEEKEEPER with 5 E's).
  • Missing Letters: Words that form a new word when you drop the first or last letter (like US state names minus one letter).
  • Spelled Backward: Words that spell another word in reverse.

Watch Out for Sneaky 5-Word Decoy Traps!

The primary way puzzle creators test your patience is the 5-word trap. An initial scan often shows five words that seem to match a theme (like five flying animals or five colors). If you guess four right away, you risk guessing the decoy word and losing one of your 4 allowed mistakes.

Golden Rule: If you spot five words that seem to fit together, pause! Set that group aside for a moment and look for a clean, separate group of four on the board.

4 Simple Steps for Every Morning Puzzle

  1. Read All 16 Words First: Read through all 16 tiles before making any guesses. Look for words that can have multiple meanings (like DATE, CLUB, or BARK).
  2. Find Words with Only One Obvious Meaning: Words with narrow, unusual meanings often help anchor the board and point you in the right direction.
  3. Pause on 5-Word Groups: If you count 5 words that seem to belong together, don't guess yet. One of those 5 is secretly needed for another group.
  4. Solve the Cleanest Group First: Locking in your most certain group removes 4 words from the board immediately, making the remaining 12 words much easier to see!

Understanding the Core Features

Connections Assistant is designed to be straightforward and distraction-free. Here is how each feature helps you solve at your own pace:

📋 Quick Paste Feature

Typing 16 separate words on a phone or tablet can be slow and clumsy. The Quick Paste button lets you paste all 16 words from a text or webpage in one simple step. It automatically puts each word into its own box for you.

🔍 Find Groups & Gentle Hints

Once your words are in the grid, click Find Groups & Gentle Hints. Instead of just giving away the answers and ruining the fun, our helper looks at how words relate to each other—checking definitions, shared themes, and clever spelling tricks—and offers gentle nudges so you can solve it yourself.

How to Master Word Grouping Puzzles

Word grouping puzzles challenge you to look at 16 words and find four groups of four related words. While it sounds simple, puzzle creators love using sneaky tricks. Here are easy ways to stay one step ahead:

Understanding Red Herrings (Decoy Words)

A "red herring" is a word placed on the board to trick your eye because it seems to fit into two different groups. For example, the word "BARK" could be a tree, a dog sound, or rough texture. Always pause before guessing to make sure you see where all the words belong!

The Four Common Ways Words Connect

To solve daily puzzles easily, look for these common connections:

  • Words That Mean the Same Thing (Synonyms): Words with shared definitions (like Leap, Jump, Bound, Spring).
  • Words That Share a Theme: Words linked by a hobby, setting, or subject (like tools found in a garden).
  • Words That Share a Common Word: Words that can come before or after another word (like Key, Snow, Surf, and Dash all pairing with "Board").
  • Fun Facts & Categories: Specific groups like "State Capitals" or "Types of Birds."

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this helper find connections?

When you enter your 16 words, our system looks at word meanings, common topics, cultural trivia, and clever spelling tricks to organize the words into their four difficulty levels: Yellow (easiest), Green, Blue, and Purple (trickiest).

Is using this helper cheating?

Not at all! Think of it like a helpful friend sitting across the breakfast table with you. Rather than blurting out the solutions, it gives gentle hints and small nudges so you still get the satisfaction of solving the puzzle yourself.
